The map (Figure A: Distribution of floods across European regions, 1995-2022, absolute number of floods per NUTS 3 region; 1,160 regions in 27 EU Member States) shows that floods have been most frequent in the Alpine arc and parts of southern Europe, while northern and much of eastern Europe recorded few or none.

Legend: 0; 1-5; 6-10; 11-15; 16-20; >20; No data.

- **Italy**: among the most flood-prone; many regions >20, especially in the north (Alps, Liguria, Po valley) and along the west coast, with 11-20 scattered through the centre and south; Sardinia and Sicily mostly 0-5.
- **Austria and Slovenia**: many regions >20 across the Alps.
- **Croatia**: coastal (Dalmatian) regions >20; inland 0-5.
- **Portugal and north-western Spain**: north Portugal and Galicia >20, northern Spanish coast 16-20, a large 11-15 area in the west of the peninsula; the rest of Spain, including the south-east (Valencia), mostly 0-5.
- **France**: mostly 0-5, but the south (Massif Central, Rhône, Alps, Pyrenees foothills) has 6-20 and some >20; Brittany tip 11-15.
- **Greece**: western mainland >20, Thessaly 11-20, Peloponnese 6-15; the north mostly 1-10.
- **Germany, Benelux, Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria**: mostly 0-5; exceptions include a 6-10 cluster (one 16-20 region) in southern Poland and an 11-15 region in northern Romania.
- **Finland, Baltics, Denmark, Cyprus**: almost entirely 0; Sweden 0-5.
- **Ireland**: north-west 6-10, most of the rest 1-5, Dublin area 0.
- **No data** (non-EU): Iceland, Norway, United Kingdom, Switzerland, Western Balkans, Turkey.

Sources: ECMWF (ERA5 dataset) and ECB staff calculations.
